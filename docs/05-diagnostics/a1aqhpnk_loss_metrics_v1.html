Three follow-ups from v5: (1) why is loss/policy negative, (2) why does loss/dyn keep rising — and what about loss/rep, (3) why do latent/prior_entropy and latent/posterior_entropy look identical.
TL;DR — three answers, three deltas
  • Q1 · loss/policy negative. The reported scalar is −w·sg(A)·log π − η·H(π). With η = 3·10−2 and H ≈ 1.32 nats, the entropy bonus alone contributes −η·H ≈ −0.04. The advantage term averages to ~0 by construction (centred & normalised). So the loss should sit near −0.04 — and it does (observed: −0.025 to −0.05). Negative is the healthy signature; positive would mean entropy collapsed.
  • Q2 · loss/dyn rising — and loss/rep is literally the same number. Verified in code (agent.py:716–754): both come from the same KL[q‖p]; only stop_gradient placement differs. loss/dyn trains the prior toward the posterior, loss/rep trains the posterior toward the prior — but the logged scalar is identical. Rising from 3.4 → 6.9 is desired: the posterior carries more observation-conditional information as the world model improves; kl_free=1 prevents collapse, not growth.
  • Q3 · prior vs. posterior entropy. They look close on the chart but they're not identical. Posterior is consistently lower (e.g., 0.32 vs. 0.45 nats early; 0.64 vs. 0.86 late). The gap is mutual information — observation removes some uncertainty in z. Both rise together because the latent space is becoming more expressive overall.

1 · Why loss/policy is negative

1.1 The actor objective (verified)

From agent.py:603–606 in the R2-Dreamer fork, the per-step actor loss is:

ℒπ = −𝔼τ∼π̂[ w · sg(A) · log π(a∣s) + η · H(π) ]

with η = act_entropy = 3e-2 (config.py:74), w the imagination-weight, A the percentile-normalised advantage. The loss is minimised, so you want the bracketed term large, i.e. you want the loss negative.

1.2 Decomposing the observed value

TermSymbolValue @ run a1aqhpnkSign of contribution to loss
Entropy bonus−η·H(π)−0.030 × 1.32 ≈ −0.040negative (large)
Policy gradient−w·sg(A)·log π(a∣s)≈ ±0.005 (noisy, mean-0)≈ 0 on average
Sum (observed)ℒπ−0.025 to −0.050matches prediction

Why the policy-gradient term averages to zero: sg(A) is the centred advantage (return minus value baseline, percentile-normalised). Over a large batch, 𝔼[A] ≈ 0, so 𝔼[A·log π] ≈ Cov(A, log π). That covariance is what actually drives learning — but the magnitude is small relative to the entropy bonus once policy and value have stabilised.

Diagnostic rule of thumb: for a healthy DreamerV3 actor with act_entropy=η and num_actions=A:
  loss/policy ≈ −η · ln(A) (uniform-policy upper bound) … here −0.03·ln 4 ≈ −0.042.
Run a1aqhpnk sits in the band [−0.05, −0.025] = ~60–95% of max-entropy. Healthy.
Run 6nn4t1ky (η=3e-4, collapsed) sits at ≈ ±10⁻⁴ ≈ −η·H with H ≈ 0.087 → entropy bonus is too small to even register; loss flips between tiny positive and negative noise.

1.3 Verdict

resolved Negative loss/policy is the expected signature of a healthy, exploring actor under DreamerV3's loss formulation. It is not evidence of negative reward; that's a different metric (episode/reward).

2 · Why loss/dyn rises — and the loss/rep identity

2.1 The two losses are the same scalar

R2-Dreamer's _kl_loss() at agent.py:716–754 computes:

dyn_loss = kl_divergence(stop_gradient(post), prior).clamp(kl_free).mean()
rep_loss = kl_divergence(post, stop_gradient(prior)).clamp(kl_free).mean()

Both are the same KL[q(z∣h,o) ‖ p(z∣h)] — only the gradient flow differs:

LossTrainsStop-grad onEffect
loss/dynprior p(z∣h)posteriorprior chases the posterior (world-model imagination)
loss/repposterior q(z∣h,o)priorposterior is regularised toward the prior (avoids overfit to obs)

The scalars are byte-identical because the KL value is the same; only the autograd graph differs. Verified across the full 1.82M-step history: every sampled row has loss/dyn === loss/rep === latent/kl_divergence to the last decimal.

2.2 Why is the KL rising?

The KL grows monotonically from ≈ 3.4 → 6.9 because:

  1. Early training: posterior q(z∣h,o) is near-uniform (encoder hasn't learned), prior p(z∣h) is also near-uniform, KL is small.
  2. As the encoder learns, the posterior becomes sharper around the observation while the prior remains image-agnostic. KL = how much information the observation adds. More learning = more KL.
  3. kl_free=1.0 sets a floor (free-bits regulariser), not a ceiling. It prevents collapse to KL=0; it does nothing to cap growth.
  4. The growth slows above 6 — the world model is approaching its representational capacity (stoch_classes=32, stoch_discrete=16 → max KL per dim ≈ ln 32 ≈ 3.47 nats; 6.9 total ≈ 0.43 per dim, well within budget).

2.3 Verdict

healthy Rising loss/dyn/loss/rep is exactly what an improving world-model should produce. Watch for: plateau before SR plateau (representational bottleneck) or sudden drop (representation collapse) — neither is happening here.

3.1 Why they look identical on the chart

Two reasons make the curves visually overlap:

  • Both metrics are mean per-dim entropies on a 0…ln 32 ≈ 3.47-nat scale. The delta (≈ 0.12–0.21 nats) is small relative to the visual axis used for the absolute values, especially if W&B auto-zooms to [0, 1].
  • They move together. When the world model becomes more expressive, both distributions become wider (less peaky) — the posterior less so because it's still pulled toward observation-conditioned modes.

3.2 What the gap actually means

For categorical posteriors, the relationship is:

H(p) − H(q) = KL[q ‖ p] − I(z; o ∣ h)

The observed gap is much smaller than the KL (0.21 vs. 6.9). That's because we report per-dim entropies for 32-class categoricals, while KL is summed across all 16 stochastic dims and 32 classes per dim. The two metrics live on different scales — comparing magnitudes directly is misleading.

What you can read off the gap:

  • Gap > 0 (always): observation reduces uncertainty in z (good — world model is using the encoder).
  • Gap = 0: posterior collapse — the encoder is being ignored. Catastrophic.
  • Gap large and growing: the encoder is becoming more discriminative. ✓ (what we see).

3.3 Verdict

healthy Posterior tracks below prior with a growing gap. Visual overlap is a chart artefact, not a metric pathology.

4 · Three to five metrics worth a closer look

v5 closed the act_entropy question. Below are the next-tier candidates — each one is either suspicious in this run, easy to misread, or a leading indicator for trouble that hasn't shown up yet. Order = priority.

① loss/barlow — Barlow-Twins SSL on encoder features

elevated Range 40–62, the largest per-step loss in the model by an order of magnitude. Coefficient is small (scale_barlow=0.05 × barlow_lambda=5e-4) so it doesn't dominate the gradient — but a stuck large loss means the SSL projector is failing to decorrelate features.

How to investigate

  1. Pull loss/barlow alongside loss/rep over the full run. If barlow climbs while rep climbs, the encoder is learning features that the projector can't decorrelate — feature redundancy. Healthy: barlow falls slowly while rep rises.
  2. Inspect agent.py:458–473: the projector head's width vs. encoder embed dim. If projector ≪ encoder, redundancy is unavoidable.
  3. Ablate by setting scale_barlow=0 for one comparison run. If SR is unchanged, the auxiliary is paying nothing.

② loss/repval — replay-value regression

stable but high Range 1.5–2.7 — comparable to loss/value (1.5–2.1). repval regresses the critic on replay features (world-model gradients on) against the imagination λ-return + slow critic. If it stays equal to or above loss/value, the slow critic is lagging the fast critic significantly — that's the signal v4 was tracking with the percentile-normaliser cold-start hypothesis.

How to investigate

  1. Plot loss/repval / loss/value ratio over training. Healthy: drops below 1.0 by 500k steps. Suspicious: stays at ≥1.0.
  2. Check slow_target_fraction=0.02 — the EMA half-life is ~35 update-steps. With train_ratio=512, that's a meaningful but short window. If repval lags, raising the slow fraction (e.g., 0.05) or shortening train_ratio is the lever.
  3. Cross-reference v5's ret/scale trace: if repval stabilises only after ret/scale stabilises, the percentile normaliser is the upstream cause.

③ loss/con — continue/done head

spiky Median is tiny (10⁻⁵), but spikes to ~10⁻² appear (e.g., step 1,380,250 = 4.3·10⁻³). The head is a sigmoid BCE on 1 − is_terminal with scale_con=1.0. ObjectNav has very sparse terminations (most steps are non-terminal); even a small absolute loss can fail to predict the rare stop action correctly.

How to investigate

  1. Pull loss/con conditioned on episode length. Spikes should cluster around episodes that end (the "1" labels). If they cluster on long episodes, the head is mispredicting non-stop steps.
  2. Confirm is_terminal vs. is_first semantics in the replay buffer (this was the v3 bug). Wrong masking gives a head that learns the buffer pattern rather than the env signal.
  3. Check imagination-time continue probabilities: low continue_prob in imagination shortens effective γ; if loss/con is mispredicting, imagination horizons collapse.

④ loss/rew — reward head

stable Range 0.23–0.28 — flat throughout. Two-hot CE on 255 symexp-spaced bins (twohot_bins=255). Stable is good… but stable around the wrong value is also possible.

How to investigate

  1. Sanity-check predicted vs. actual reward histograms in imagination. Pull train/imagined_reward if logged; otherwise, do a manual decode of the head over a replay batch.
  2. Confirm the bin range covers ObjectNav's reward distribution. Habitat's slack reward + success bonus puts most rewards in [−1, +10]; a 255-bin symexp spans ±20 ≈ ±e³, so it should resolve fine.
  3. Compare loss/rew magnitude against the entropy of the reward distribution — flat low loss + low entropy data = head outputs the marginal. Run the head on a held-out batch and check Brier score.

⑤ kl/prior + kl/post (the unclipped KL halves, if logged separately)

The current logging pipeline reports the clipped KL as loss/dyn = loss/rep. The unclipped halves (the "free bits not yet activated" portion) are useful: they show how often the kl_free=1.0 floor is binding. agent.py:439–444 logs kl/prior and kl/post as metrics. Worth pulling.

How to investigate

  1. Pull kl/prior and kl/post for run a1aqhpnk. If both stay above 1.0 throughout, free-bits is irrelevant (good). If either drops below 1.0 frequently, that's where the floor matters — and where small kl_free tweaks would change behaviour.
  2. Cross-plot against latent/kl_divergence to see clip activation rate.
