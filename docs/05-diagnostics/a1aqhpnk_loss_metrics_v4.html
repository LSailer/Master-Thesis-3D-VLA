TL;DR — three answers, one A/B plan
  • Entropy bonus is the regulariser that keeps the policy alive. The only force pushing on $\pi$ when $A \approx 0$. Without it, the actor freezes on whichever action got an early lucky positive $A$.
  • Policy gradient is the direction of learning. Magnitude is small by design — advantages are centred and percentile-normalised. Direction is what matters, not magnitude.
  • Three identical metrics? Forward values are byte-identical (same KL[q‖p]). But $\mathrm{scale\_dyn}=1.0$ vs $\mathrm{scale\_rep}=0.1$ means gradient contributions differ 10×. latent/kl_divergence is purely cosmetic — issue #143 filed.

1 · The entropy bonus — what it does, visually

1.1 Two distinct jobs in the actor loss

actor loss · KaTeX-rendered
$$ \mathcal{L}_\pi \;=\; \underbrace{-\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\, w \cdot \mathrm{sg}(\hat A) \cdot \log \pi(a \mid s) \,\right]}_{\textcolor{#2a9a4a}{\textsf{policy gradient — direction}}} \;\; \underbrace{-\;\eta \cdot H(\pi)}_{\textcolor{#c47c1a}{\textsf{entropy bonus — temperature}}} $$
Mental model. Imagine driving a car. The policy gradient is the steering wheel — it tells you which way to go, but only when you have information (advantage). The entropy bonus is the gas pedal — it keeps you moving even when you don't know where to steer. Without the gas pedal, you stop. Without the steering wheel, you crash into whatever's directly ahead.

1.2 Why a 4-action env needs 100× more entropy than a 17-action env

entropy ceiling
$$ H_{\max} \;=\; \ln |\mathcal{A}|, \qquad H_{\text{Habitat}} \le \ln 4 \approx 1.39, \quad H_{\text{Crafter}} \le \ln 17 \approx 2.83 $$
The thesis-paragraph version (now in content/Appendix.tex:12–14): "The actor entropy bonus $\eta \cdot H(\pi)$ acts as a regularizer rather than a primary objective: it prevents the policy from collapsing to a single action before the world model has seen enough trajectories. It is the only force keeping $\pi(a \mid s)$ stochastic when the advantage signal $A \approx 0$…"

2 · The policy gradient — small magnitude is by design

2.1 Why $\mathbb{E}[\hat A] \approx 0$ by construction

percentile-normalised advantage (DreamerV3 §3)
$$ \hat A \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{sg}\!\bigl(R_\lambda - V_\psi(s)\bigr)}{\max\!\bigl(1,\; \mathrm{Per}_{95}(R_\lambda) - \mathrm{Per}_{5}(R_\lambda)\bigr)} $$

2.2 The asymmetry of jobs

Policy gradient Entropy bonus
Magnitudenoisy, mean ≈ 0steady, large
Information content"which way to go""don't commit yet"
Cumulative effectdirectional drift over many batchesrestoring force back to uniform
If you remove itpolicy stays uniform — no learningpolicy collapses to argmax of early noise — locked-in bad policy

3 · Why three logs for one number

forward — same scalar; backward — different gradient routes
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{dyn}} \;=\; \mathrm{KL}\!\bigl[\,\mathrm{sg}(q) \,\big\|\, p\,\bigr], \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{rep}} \;=\; \mathrm{KL}\!\bigl[\,q \,\big\|\, \mathrm{sg}(p)\,\bigr] $$ $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} \;\supset\; \underbrace{1.0 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{dyn}}}_{\text{trains prior}} \;+\; \underbrace{0.1 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{rep}}}_{\text{trains encoder}} $$
