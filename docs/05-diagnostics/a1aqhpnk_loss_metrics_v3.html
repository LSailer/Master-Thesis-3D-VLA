TL;DR — three answers, one A/B plan
  • Entropy bonus = the regularizer that keeps the policy alive. The only force pushing on π when the advantage signal A ≈ 0. Without it, the actor freezes on whichever action got an early lucky positive A.
  • Policy gradient = the direction of learning. Magnitude is small by design — advantages are centred and percentile-normalised. Direction is what matters, not magnitude.
  • Three identical metrics? Forward values are byte-identical (same KL[q‖p]). But scale_dyn=1.0 vs scale_rep=0.1 means gradient contributions differ 10×. latent/kl_divergence is purely cosmetic — issue #143 filed to drop it.

1 · The entropy bonus — what it does, visually

1.1 Two distinct jobs in the actor loss

ℒπ = −𝔼[ w · sg(A) · log π(a∣s) ]  −  η · H(π)
[ policy gradient — direction ]   [ entropy bonus — temperature ]
Mental model. Imagine driving a car. The policy gradient is the steering wheel — it tells you which way to go, but only when you have information (advantage). The entropy bonus is the gas pedal — it keeps you moving even when you don't know where to steer. Without the gas pedal, you stop. Without the steering wheel, you crash into whatever's directly ahead.

1.2 Why a 4-action env needs 100× more entropy than a 17-action env

The thesis-paragraph version (now in content/Appendix.tex:12–14): "The actor entropy bonus η·H(π) acts as a regularizer rather than a primary objective: it prevents the policy from collapsing to a single action before the world model has seen enough trajectories. It is the only force keeping π(a∣s) stochastic when the advantage signal A ≈ 0…"
