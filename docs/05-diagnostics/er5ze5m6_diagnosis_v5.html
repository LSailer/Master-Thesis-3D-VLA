Diagnosis v5 — act_entropy 3e-2 vs 3e-4: the experiment

v4 left this as the open empirical question. Two A/B runs settled it. a1aqhpnk  ·  3e-2 6nn4t1ky  ·  3e-4 issue-142 metrics now logged

TL;DR — the paper default 3e-4 collapses the policy on Habitat 4-action ObjectNav

  • Same code, same env, same optimizer, same architecture; only act_entropy (and seed) differ. Both ran ~17.4 h on the same hardware to ~1.74 M steps.
  • Success rate: 3e-2 → SR 0.74 (peak 0.82). 3e-4 → SR 0.16, after a brief flirt with 0.45 around step 700 k that regressed.
  • Entropy: 3e-2 stays at ~1.32 nats (max for 4 actions = ln 4 ≈ 1.386, so ≈ 95 % of max). 3e-4 collapsed to ~0.087 by step 250 k and never recovered. This is the smoking gun.
  • Action distribution at the end: 3e-2 is balanced (forward 28 % / left 23 % / right 32 % / stop 17 %). 3e-4 is a left-spinner (forward 26 % / left 65 % / right 1.2 % / stop 7.6 %).
  • Value head runs away on the 3e-4 side: actor/ret_scale climbs from ~16 to 91, return_mean climbs to 56 while real episode reward stays negative (mean −1.31). The bootstrap target inflates a self-consistent fantasy because the policy never explores.
  • Policy gradient is dead: loss/policy oscillates around ≈ ±1·10⁻⁴ on the 3e-4 run vs. a healthy ≈ −0.04 on the 3e-2 run. A near-deterministic actor with stable returns means 𝔼[A · log π] ≈ 0; the only thing that could reopen the policy is the entropy bonus, and at η = 3·10⁻⁴ it is too small.
  • Reference check (R2-Dreamer fork): 3e-4 is the canonical paper / parity value (modules/r2dreamer/launch/parity/batch_utils.py:90); the fork's argparse default is 3e-2 with an inline comment calling it the "Habitat 4-action ObjectNav baseline" precisely because 3e-4 "collapses the policy here" (modules/r2dreamer/launch/parser.py:30). v5 confirms that comment empirically, with metrics.

3 — Entropy: the policy collapses early

Actor entropy in the policy distribution is −Σ p log p. With four actions it is bounded above by ln 4 ≈ 1.386. Both runs start near-uniform; in the 3e-4 run, the collapse happens before the first metric is logged — the earliest sample we have is at step 253 k and entropy is already at 0.08.

Entropy vs argmax probability (mapping)

For a 4-way categorical with argmax mass p and uniform tail, entropy is H(p) = −p ln p − 3·((1−p)/3)·ln((1−p)/3). H = 0.087 ⇒ p ≈ 0.974. H = 1.32 ⇒ p ≈ 0.30 (close to uniform). So the 3e-4 actor takes its preferred action 97 % of the time; the 3e-2 actor takes its preferred action 30 % of the time — proper exploration.

5 — The value head runs away to a self-consistent fantasy

This is the most interesting part. Once the policy collapses, the world model only ever sees one trajectory family (the spin), so the actor's bootstrapped target r + γ·V' stabilises — but at a value completely disconnected from the actual environment reward. The percentile normaliser S = max(1, Per95 − Per5) tracks that target, and the actor optimisation continues against the inflated scale.

6 — The policy gradient is dead

The actor loss in r2dreamer (cf. modules/r2dreamer/agent.py as audited in v4) is roughly

ℒπ  =  − w · A · log π(a|s)  −  η · H(π)

With η = 3·10⁻⁴, a near-deterministic policy, and a self-consistent value head:

  • The advantage is small: actor/advantage_std = 0.023 on 3e-4 vs 0.059 on 3e-2 (the spread shrinks ~2.5×). The world is locked into one trajectory shape, so r + γV' − V is small.
  • The entropy term is small: η·H ≈ 3·10⁻⁴ · 0.087 = 2.6·10⁻⁵. That is what is left of the gradient that could push the policy back toward exploration.
  • The total loss is essentially zero:

The system is in a local fixed point that the optimiser cannot escape.

8 — Why DreamerV3's paper default doesn't transfer here

Hafner et al. tuned η = 3·10⁻⁴ on Crafter (17 discrete actions) and on procedural game environments where the actor benefits from extrinsic-reward density. Two relevant differences for 4-action Habitat ObjectNav:

  1. 17 actions vs 4 actions. The maximum entropy for 4 actions is ln 4 ≈ 1.39; for 17 actions it is ln 17 ≈ 2.83. Relative contribution of η·H vs A·log π in DreamerV3's loss scales with both H and the per-action gradient mass. Cutting from 17 to 4 actions shrinks the maximum entropy bonus by ≈ 2× before any collapse.
  2. Reward sparsity profile. Crafter rewards are dense achievement-based (~22 unlock targets, mostly small positive). ObjectNav-chair gives a single +10 at episode end and a continuous −0.01 per step. The advantage signal is only large for ~0.05 % of steps — the rest of the time, the gradient on log π is essentially noise. With η = 3·10⁻⁴ there is nothing to keep the actor exploring during those long quiet stretches.
  3. Percentile normaliser cold-start. v3 already noted that S = max(1, Per95 − Per5) initialises at S = 1 when both percentiles are zeros. That is fine — but it means early-training advantages are not down-weighted, so any small-but-systematic preference (e.g. "left turn looked good in the first batch") gets amplified. Combined with low η, this is the recipe for the 250 k-step entropy collapse we observe.

None of these undermine the paper — they explain why the same hyperparameter does not auto-transfer to a 4-action sparse-terminal-reward environment.
