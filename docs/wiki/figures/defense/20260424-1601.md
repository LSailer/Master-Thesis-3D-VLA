## Braun (Erstprüfer) — Neuroinformatics / Bayesian / world-model foundations

### Q1: Warum World Models statt einer Diffusion Policy?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/meetings/2026-03-03-braun.md`
**Answer:** Diffusion policies predict action chunks but do not model environment dynamics. A world model (RSSM) explicitly learns the transition structure of the environment, enabling look-ahead planning via imagined rollouts and better sample efficiency. The pivot was motivated specifically to make the 2D-vs-3D question falsifiable: with a world model we can ask whether 3D inputs improve *predicted dynamics*, not just action-chunk quality. Without a world model, "does 3D help" collapses into "does 3D help map obs → action," which conflates perception and control.

### Q2: What does your model "believe" about the world it has not seen?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/methods/world-model-training-loop.md`
**Answer:** The RSSM maintains a stochastic latent state (16 × 32 discrete) and a deterministic GRU-style state (2048). Beliefs about unseen states come from the prior: given the current latent and a chosen action, the prior predicts the next latent distribution *without* seeing the next observation. On L1, the prior's KL divergence from the posterior grows 2.9 → 6.0 during training (`l1-baseline-2.4m.md` Finding 4), meaning the posterior extracts more than the prior can predict — yet the agent still reaches 67 % SR, suggesting the prior is "good enough" for policy optimization even when imperfect.

### Q3: Why 64-step windows for world-model training and 15-step imagination for policy?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/methods/world-model-training-loop.md`
**Answer:** 64 steps captures all local transition dynamics ("FORWARD advances", "LEFT rotates") without requiring padding for variable-length episodes — fixed shape enables JAX JIT compilation. 15-step imagination is the policy's planning horizon; the actor learns actions that maximize imagined return over 15 steps, and hundreds of locally optimal 15-step decisions compose into coherent 200+ step navigation (mean L1 episode ~250 steps).

### Q4: The val dyn loss is 42, train dyn loss is 6. Isn't the model just overfitting?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/experiments/l1-baseline-2.4m.md` Finding 2
**Answer:** Yes, the world model overfits to the training trajectory distribution in the single-house setting — 7,499 starting positions repeatedly cycled over 2.4 M steps. But SR still reaches 67 % because the policy uses imagination from current observation states, not held-out ones. On L3 (10 houses) the val dyn loss drops to 27 — scene diversity forces more general dynamics. The overfitting is a symptom of L1's constraint, not a failure mode.

### Q5: When you say the model "dreams", what exactly is being dreamed?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/methods/world-model-training-loop.md`
**Answer:** At every training step the agent samples latent states from a 64-step replay window, then rolls 15 steps forward in *latent space* using only the RSSM prior — no encoder, no real observation. Each rolled-out state produces a predicted reward and continuation. The actor-critic trains on imagined returns from these rollouts, never on real replay transitions directly. So "dreaming" is a sequence of prior-predicted latent states conditioned only on actions.

### Q6: Is a discrete 16×32 latent expressive enough for a 3D scene?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/methods/world-model-training-loop.md` (config), `docs/wiki/experiments/l1-baseline-2.4m.md`
**Answer:** 16 categorical variables × 32 classes = 10²⁰+ representable states per timestep. The question is not capacity but disentanglement. DreamerV3 found discrete latents more stable than continuous Gaussians on long-horizon tasks. On L1 the agent reaches 67 % with this capacity; the next test is L4 / VGGT, where richer 3D input might require more stochastic units — this is an open empirical question, not a theoretical constraint.

### Q7: What does VGGT give the model that the CNN does not?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/methods/l4-profiling.md` (VGGT pipeline), `docs/wiki/meetings/2026-03-03-braun.md`
**Answer:** VGGT produces geometric features from multi-view aggregation (camera head + point head), giving the agent implicit access to depth and 3D structure from a single RGB stream. The CNN sees only 64×64 RGB. The hypothesis is that 3D-aware features help the world model predict scene dynamics that depend on occupancy and free space — not just pixel appearance. Whether this is actually what the agent uses is a gap (see Gap 5 above).

### Q8: If the policy is stochastic, how do you handle epistemic vs aleatoric uncertainty?

**Anchor:** no direct wiki evidence — answer from architecture
**Answer:** The RSSM mixes both implicitly: the stochastic latent is a Bayesian-ish posterior over current state given observations; the actor's entropy term encourages exploration under ambiguity; the KL regularizer between prior and posterior bounds representation drift. There is no explicit Bayesian uncertainty quantification beyond this. This is a **real limitation for the thesis** and a gap to flag honestly in the defense.

### Q9: Your validation dyn loss behavior is monotonically worsening (17 → 42). Is this a sign the world model is confused?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/experiments/l1-baseline-2.4m.md` Finding 2
**Answer:** Not confusion — specialization. The model is learning the exact trajectory distribution of the 7,499 training starting positions, so validation episodes from different positions look increasingly "off-distribution" in its eyes. The train loss also rises (2.9 → 6.0) but less steeply. The gap quantifies training-set memorization vs general dynamics. On L3 (10 houses) the gap narrows (6.9 vs 27.5) — consistent with forced generalization.

### Q10: What would falsify your thesis?

**Anchor:** no existing wiki evidence — this is a gap
**Answer:** A negative result on the primary comparison: if L4-VGGT (full curriculum with 3D features) does not outperform L4-CNN on held-out scenes, or matches within noise, the claim "3D features help world models for ObjectNav" is falsified for this setup. **Gap:** the falsification criterion is not yet stated in any wiki page; it should be added to a top-level `methods/thesis-hypotheses.md` before the defense.

So "does 3D help" is specifically the question: does an encoder that already knows geometry outperform one that has to learn it from RGB + reward.

### Q8: The L3 val dyn loss is *lower* than L1 (27 vs 40). Standard reading: less overfitting. Alternative: the model hasn't learned house-specific dynamics at all and is averaging. How do you distinguish these?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/experiments/l3-10houses-chair.md` Finding 2
**Answer:** The distinction is testable: if the model has learned *general* dynamics, per-house SR should be relatively uniform (possibly with hard-house degradation). If it has merely averaged, SR on *any* house should be low and uniform, close to the cross-house mean. Per-house SR (Gap 6) directly discriminates these. Current wiki cannot answer this yet.

### Q9: Your KV-cache reset hook fires on `env.reset()`. What about mid-episode scene boundaries — e.g., the agent entering a new room through a door?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/methods/l4-profiling.md` KV-cache audit
**Answer:** The current design resets the cache only at episode boundaries — 9 resets ↔ 9 episode starts in the profiling run. Mid-episode room transitions are NOT reset; VGGT accumulates context across the entire episode via the padded 3-tuple cache (see `methods/vggt-jax-streaming.md`). This is a design choice: cross-room context may help the world model, but may also inject stale geometry for occluded regions. Not validated empirically.

### Q10: The test suite pins bit-parity at atol 1e-5 for fp32 padded cache. The JAX port shows maxabs 1.1e-2 in bf16. How do you know the bf16 path is correct, not just "close enough"?

**Anchor:** `docs/wiki/methods/vggt-jax-streaming.md` Deferred section
**Answer:** The wiki acknowledges this: "current L1 dump shows maxabs 1.1e-2 (rel_err 5e-3) PT↔JAX, which is the bf16 streaming-attention precision band. If a downstream metric turns out to be sensitive to <1e-2 drift, revisit." The fp32 path is bit-exact; the bf16 path is inside the expected numerical precision band for streaming attention. No downstream metric has yet shown sensitivity below 1e-2. Acceptable but explicitly provisional.
