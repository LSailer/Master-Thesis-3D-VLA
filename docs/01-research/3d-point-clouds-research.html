1A 9-year timeline at a glance

How the field collapsed from "many specialized stages" into "single feed-forward transformers."

2017PointNet — first deep network on raw point sets via permutation-invariant max pooling.
2018–19DGCNN, KPConv, MinkowskiNet, VoxelNet, SECOND — graphs, kernel-point conv, sparse 3D conv, voxel detectors.
2020Point Transformer v1 — self-attention beats convolutions on point clouds. NeRF reframes 3D as a function in network weights.
2021–22Point-BERT, Point-MAE — masked-modeling pretraining. PointNeXt shows training recipe rivals new ops.
20233D Gaussian Splatting ships — explicit, fast, photorealistic. ULIP / Point-LLM bring CLIP and LLMs to 3D.
2024DUSt3R / MASt3R, Uni3D, PTv3 — feed-forward 3D from RGB without intrinsics, billion-param 3D encoders, scalable transformer backbone.
2025VGGT (CVPR Best Paper) — single transformer outputs cameras + depth + pointmaps + tracks in <1s. UNITE adds semantics + open-vocabulary + affordances on top. ← your stack.

2Foundational architectures

The classical "point-based" lineage. Permutation invariance is the key inductive bias: a network $f$ over a point set $\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ must satisfy $f(\pi \cdot X) = f(X)$ for any permutation $\pi$. PointNet enforces this via $f(X) = \rho(\max_i h(x_i))$ — a shared MLP $h$ followed by a symmetric pool.

PointNet Must read

Qi et al. · CVPR 2017 · arXiv:1612.00593

First deep network on raw, unordered point sets. Per-point shared MLP + symmetric max-pool, plus T-Net alignment.

Why it matters: Founded the entire point-based branch of 3D learning; the conceptual baseline every later method compares to.

arXiv

PointNet++ Must read

Qi et al. · NeurIPS 2017 · arXiv:1706.02413

Hierarchical local features via farthest-point sampling, ball-query neighborhoods, PointNet sub-networks at each scale.

Why it matters: Fixed PointNet's blindness to local geometry; the FPS + group + PointNet template is still the default building block.

arXiv

DGCNN

Wang et al. · TOG 2019 · arXiv:1801.07829

EdgeConv: graph conv on a k-NN graph that is dynamically recomputed in feature space at every layer.

Why it matters: Showed that semantic neighbors (not just spatial ones) carry useful structure.

arXiv

KPConv

Thomas et al. · ICCV 2019 · arXiv:1904.08889

Kernel Point Convolution — weights live at continuous kernel points in Euclidean space. Deformable variant adapts kernel locations.

Why it matters: Made true convolutions on raw points work without voxelization; dominated outdoor segmentation for years.

arXiv

Point Transformer v1

Zhao, Jiang, Jia, Torr, Koltun · ICCV 2021 · arXiv:2012.09164

Pure self-attention backbone with vector self-attention and learnable positional encodings on local k-NN neighborhoods.

Why it matters: First strong evidence that transformers — not convs or graphs — are the right primitive for points.

arXiv

Point Transformer v2

Wu et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · arXiv:2210.05666

Grouped vector attention, additional positional-encoding multiplier, partition-based pooling.

Why it matters: Bridged the gap between PTv1's expressivity and the efficiency needed for large scenes.

arXiv

Point Transformer v3 Must read

Wu et al. · CVPR 2024 Oral · arXiv:2312.10035

"Simpler, faster, stronger." Replaces precise k-NN with serialized neighbor mapping (Z-order / Hilbert). 3× faster, 10× more memory-efficient than PTv2.

Why it matters: Currently the default high-end backbone for 3D segmentation. SOTA on 20+ benchmarks.

arXiv

PointNeXt

Qian et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · arXiv:2206.04670

Demonstrates that modern training recipes (augmentation, optimization, schedule) close most of the gap to fancier architectures, on top of plain PointNet++.

Why it matters: Important sanity check — training tricks rival new ops. Always have this baseline in mind before designing a new model.

arXiv

MinkowskiNet (Sparse Conv)

Choy et al. · CVPR 2019 · arXiv:1904.08755

Generalized sparse convolutions in arbitrary dimensions (incl. 4D spatio-temporal). Auto-diff sparse-tensor library underlying most modern 3D segmentation pipelines.

arXiv

VoxelNet

Zhou & Tuzel · CVPR 2018 · arXiv:1711.06396

End-to-end LiDAR detector: voxelize → Voxel Feature Encoding (mini-PointNet per voxel) → 3D conv → RPN. First fully learned point-cloud detector without hand-crafted features.

arXiv

SECOND

Yan et al. · Sensors 2018

Sparse 3D conv + angle-loss regression + GT-paste augmentation. Made voxel detectors fast (20–40 fps) and viable for production AV stacks.

paper

PV-RCNN

Shi et al. · CVPR 2020 · arXiv:1912.13192

Two-stage detector fusing voxel-CNN features with PointNet-style set abstraction at keypoints, then RoI-grid pooling.

Why it matters: Combined voxel efficiency with point precision; long-time SOTA on KITTI / Waymo and template behind many follow-ups.

arXiv

3Foundation models & large-scale pretraining

2021–2026 — the "GPT moment" for 3D. Self-supervised pretraining, multimodal alignment, billion-parameter encoders.

Self-supervised pretraining

Point-BERT Must read

Yu et al. · CVPR 2022 · arXiv:2111.14819

BERT-style Masked Point Modeling — a dVAE tokenizer produces discrete tokens; transformer recovers masked tokens. 93.8% on ModelNet40.

arXiv

Point-MAE Must read

Pang et al. · ECCV 2022 · arXiv:2203.06604

Direct port of MAE: high mask ratio, asymmetric encoder/decoder, reconstruct coordinates. Often stronger than Point-BERT and far simpler.

arXiv

Point-M2AE

Zhang et al. · NeurIPS 2022 · arXiv:2205.14401

Multi-scale, hierarchical MAE with pyramid encoder/decoder — captures both fine geometry and high-level semantics.

arXiv

PointGPT

Chen et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · arXiv:2305.11487

Auto-regressive GPT-style pretraining: orders patches by spatial proximity, predicts the next patch with a dual-masking extractor-generator.

arXiv

Multimodal alignment (3D ↔ image ↔ text)

ULIP / ULIP-2

Xue et al. · CVPR 2023 / 2024 · arXiv:2212.05171, 2305.08275

Aligns point-cloud encoders into the CLIP image–text space. ULIP-2 uses LMM-generated captions instead of manual labels.

Why it matters: First strong zero-shot 3D classification; backbone of many open-vocabulary 3D systems.

ULIP-2

Uni3D Must read

Zhou et al. · ICLR 2024 Spotlight · arXiv:2310.06773

Scales a 2D-initialized ViT to a 1B-parameter unified 3D encoder aligned with CLIP-style features. SOTA on zero-shot, few-shot, open-world, and part-segmentation.

Why it matters: Demonstrates that 2D-style scaling laws transfer to 3D. Closest analogue to your "VGGT/UNITE as feature extractor" framing on the encoder side.

arXiv

Point-Bind & Point-LLM

Guo et al. · 2023 · arXiv:2309.00615

Aligns point clouds with image, language, audio, video in an ImageBind-style joint space. First 3D-aware instruction-following LLM.

arXiv

PointLLM

Xu et al. · ECCV 2024 · arXiv:2308.16911

Empowers LLMs to consume colored object point clouds directly with a 730K-sample point-text instruction dataset.

arXiv

4Feed-forward 3D from images ← your area

The DUSt3R → MASt3R → VGGT → UNITE lineage replaces classical SfM/MVS+segmentation pipelines with single, learned, feed-forward transformers. This is what enables 3D features to drop into RL / world-model loops at all.

DUSt3R Lineage

Wang et al. · CVPR 2024 · arXiv:2312.14132

Dense Unconstrained Stereo 3D Reconstruction: regresses per-image pointmaps in a shared frame from a pair of uncalibrated images — no intrinsics, no poses required.

Why it matters: Reframed multi-view geometry as direct regression — the conceptual ancestor of VGGT.

arXiv

MASt3R Lineage

Leroy et al. · ECCV 2024 · arXiv:2406.09756

DUSt3R + a local feature head, metric pointmaps, and scalable global alignment for matching/SfM at thousands-of-image scale.

Why it matters: Adds dense matching — relevant for downstream localization in your nav setting.

arXiv

VGGT Your stack CVPR 2025 Best Paper

Wang, Chen, Karaev, Vedaldi, Rupprecht, Novotny · Oxford VGG / Meta AI · arXiv:2503.11651

Single feed-forward transformer that infers all key 3D attributes — camera params, depth maps, point maps, 3D point tracks — from 1 to hundreds of views in <1 s. Beats optimization-based pipelines.

Why it matters for you: Unified 3D feature extractor that fits naturally into a world-model loop like DreamerV3 — no SfM/MVS pre-processing needed.

arXivCVPR oral

UNITE Your stack

Dec 2025 · arXiv:2512.14364

From RGB only, jointly predicts a 3D point reconstruction with dense features, semantic + instance segmentation, open-vocabulary descriptors, and affordance / articulation signals. Trained via 2D-foundation-model distillation + multi-view consistency loss.

Why it matters for you: Conceptually a "VGGT for semantics." The segmentation + open-vocab + affordance heads are directly useful for ObjectNav.

arXivproject page

3D Gaussian Splatting

Kerbl, Kopanas, Leimkühler, Drettakis · SIGGRAPH 2023 · arXiv:2308.04079

Anisotropic 3D Gaussians (essentially oriented, scaled, color-bearing "fat points") rasterized via differentiable splatting. ≥30 fps at 1080p, SOTA quality.

Relation to point clouds: 3DGS = a rich-attribute point cloud (each point carries opacity, scale, rotation, SH color). Treat it as an extension, not a separate field.

arXiv

Review: Feed-forward 3D Reconstruction

Jul 2025 · arXiv:2507.08448

Specifically traces the DUSt3R → MASt3R → MV-DUSt3R+ → VGGT lineage you're building on.

arXiv

5Application domains

3D Object Detection (Autonomous Driving)

Benchmarks: KITTI (15k frames), nuScenes (40k frames, NDS metric), Waymo Open (200k frames).

Dominant families: voxel/sparse-conv (SECOND, CenterPoint), point-voxel hybrids (PV-RCNN), and increasingly transformer-based (PTv3-Extreme won the 2024 Waymo semantic-seg challenge — arXiv:2407.15282).

3D Semantic / Instance Segmentation

Indoor: S3DIS (272 scenes, 13 classes) and ScanNet / ScanNet200 (~1.6k scans).

Current SOTA: PTv3 backbones + Mask3D-style decoders; OneFormer3D (CVPR 2024) unifies semantic / instance / panoptic.

3D Scene Understanding / Reconstruction Your area

DUSt3R → MASt3R → VGGT → UNITE replaces classical SfM/MVS+segmentation with feed-forward 3D + semantics from posed-or-unposed RGB.

Enables 3D features to drop into downstream policies and world models without per-scene optimization.

Robotics / Embodied AI / Navigation Your platform

Habitat + HM3D (1,000 building-scale reconstructions, 112.5k m² navigable space). HM3D-Sem adds dense semantics; HM3D-OVON provides open-vocabulary ObjectNav with 379 categories.

Standard sandbox for pretrained 3D features (VGGT/UNITE) inside agents like DreamerV3.

HM3D HM3D-Sem HM3D-OVON

3D Generation

Diffusion on point clouds (Point-E, Shap-E), large reconstruction models (LRM, Instant3D), Gaussian-splat generators (DreamGaussian, LGM).

Increasingly converges with the feed-forward reconstruction models above.

Guo, Wang, Hu, Liu, Liu, Bennamoun · IEEE TPAMI · arXiv:1912.12033

Classic, still-cited reference for the pre-foundation-model era. Covers classification, detection/tracking, segmentation.

8Books

Multiple View Geometry in Computer Vision

Hartley & Zisserman · Cambridge Univ. Press · 2nd ed. 2004 · ISBN 978-0521540513

The canonical reference for projective geometry, epipolar geometry, bundle adjustment.

How to use it: Read chapters on projective cameras, two-view geometry, and bundle adjustment to understand what VGGT/DUSt3R/MASt3R are learning to replace.

companion site

Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications

Szeliski · Springer · 2nd ed. 2022 · free PDF

Broader and gentler than Hartley & Zisserman.

Read: Chapter 11 (SfM), 12 (depth), 13 (3D reconstruction).

free PDF

Geometric Deep Learning free

Bronstein, Bruna, Cohen, Veličković · 2021 · arXiv:2104.13478

Free book-length treatment giving a unified framework (symmetry, equivariance) for CNNs, GNNs, transformers — and explains why permutation-invariant architectures like PointNet are the right inductive bias.

arXivsite

Airborne and Terrestrial Laser Scanning

Vosselman & Maas (eds.) · Whittles · 2010 · ISBN 978-1904445876

Pre-deep-learning reference for sensor physics, registration, filtering, segmentation of LiDAR.

Skip if: You only work with rendered HM3D depth — but useful background if you ever touch real sensor data.

publisher

Open3D, PCL, PyTorch3D documentation

11Suggested reading order (for someone starting now)

  1. Skim Guo 2020 survey (arXiv:1912.12033) for the landscape — 1 hour.
  2. Read PointNet + PointNet++ + DGCNN in one sitting — these three give you the core mental model.
  3. Read Point-MAE (the readable SSL paper) and the arXiv:2305.04691 SSL survey to understand pretraining.
  4. Read DUSt3R → MASt3R → VGGT → UNITE in order — that's the lineage your thesis sits in.
  5. Read Irshad 2024 Neural Fields in Robotics (arXiv:2410.20220) and Raychaudhuri 2025 (arXiv:2501.05750) for how 3D representations plug into navigation.
  6. Pick chapters from Hartley & Zisserman or Szeliski only when you need a specific geometric concept (e.g., epipolar geometry when explaining what VGGT replaces).
  7. Watch Justin Johnson's UMich Lecture 17 + Sitzmann's TUM AI talk as one evening's worth of consolidation.
  8. For depth: pick chapters from CMU 16-825 course slides matching whatever you need next.
