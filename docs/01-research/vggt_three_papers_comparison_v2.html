1. 🔍 Direct answers to your seven questions

Q1. How does VGGT-World use camera positions?

It doesn't take them as input.

VGGT-World is action-free and pose-free. The flow transformer $F_\theta$ is conditioned only on previously-rolled-out latent chunks (Eq. 2 of the paper):

$$\mathbf{c}_i = (\mathbf{Z}_{i-k},\ldots,\mathbf{Z}_{i-1})$$

No extrinsics, no ego-motion deltas, no viewpoint embeddings are concatenated. What does happen with the camera:

  • The camera token (one of the 5 special tokens at $d=1024$, layer 4 of the aggregator) rides along with patch + register tokens — the flow transformer treats it indistinguishably and predicts its future values jointly with everything else.
  • At decode time the predicted camera-token slot is passed through VGGT's frozen camera head, which yields predicted future camera parameters.
  • This is how their ATE/RTE/RRE numbers are produced (Sec. 4.3): "we predict camera parameters from the generated VGGT features and compare them against ground truth trajectories." So camera trajectory is a prediction target evaluated as a side-effect, not a control input.
  • The authors flag this in Appendix 0.D as a known limitation: "this minimal conditioning limits the degree of precise control over the generation process."

Implication for your DreamerV3 use: VGGT-World can't be plugged in as a controllable world model. Your RSSM already does action conditioning — that's exactly the gap they leave open.

Q2. "I understand that MG-Nav uses the 1024 + 5 dimensions from VGGT" — is this correct?

No — that's VGGT-World, not MG-Nav.

You're conflating the two papers. Here's the disentangling:

PatternWhat it meansWhich paper actually does this
$N_p + 5$ tokens at $d=1024$ per frameThe full bundle: patch tokens + 1 camera token + 4 register tokens, kept as a sequenceVGGT-World only. They tap layer 4 of the aggregator and keep all $N_p+5$ tokens.
Spatial-average-pooled feature mapVGGT's patch grid pooled to a smaller grid, flattened to a short token sequenceMG-Nav. Camera and register tokens are not included.
Single $d{=}64$ vector after pooling + MLPOne scalar-equivalent condition per frame, joint over views and tokensVGGT-DP. Includes the camera token in the average but pools it away.

MG-Nav §3.4.1 verbatim: "we use VGGT to extract geometry-aware feature maps from both the current observation $\mathbf{I}_\mathrm{obs}$ and the visual goal $\mathbf{I}_\mathrm{goal}$. After applying spatial average pooling, the resulting feature maps are flattened into token sequences $\mathbf{F}^G_\mathrm{obs}$ and $\mathbf{F}^G_\mathrm{goal}$."

The exact post-pool grid size is not stated in the v1 paper — the appendix is referenced but missing from the arXiv release. So we know the operation, not the dimensions.

Q3. Does MG-Nav use mean pool for VGGT?

Yes — explicitly "spatial average pooling".

The paper uses the verbatim phrase "spatial average pooling" in §3.4.1. No formula is given. The natural interpretation given the wording "feature maps … flattened into token sequences" is:

  1. Take the $N_p$ patch tokens of dim 1024.
  2. Reshape them to the 2D patch grid (e.g., $H_p \times W_p \times 1024$).
  3. Apply spatial average pooling to a smaller grid (e.g., $7\times7\times1024$).
  4. Flatten that smaller grid back to a token sequence (e.g., $49 \times 1024$).

So pooling reduces spatial resolution but probably preserves the channel dim. Camera and register tokens have no spatial layout, so they cannot be "spatially pooled" — they're effectively dropped. This is consistent with the paper never mentioning them.

Q4. How does VGGT-DP use FiLM conditioning?

Inherited from standard DP — paper does not detail it.

VGGT-DP says only:

"The U-Net backbone uses a downsampling structure with channel dimensions and applies FiLM (Perez et al. 2018) conditioning for integrating observation features."

So they cite Perez et al. (2018) for the formulation and Chi et al. (2023, the Diffusion Policy paper) for the U-Net-1D, but do not specify:

  • which ResBlocks or depths get FiLM,
  • which sub-network produces the affine parameters $\gamma$ and $\beta$,
  • whether the timestep embedding is fused with the VGGT condition or kept separate.

The standard Diffusion Policy reference design (which they almost certainly inherit) is:

$$\mathrm{FiLM}(x \mid \gamma,\beta) = \gamma \odot x + \beta,\qquad \gamma,\beta = \mathrm{MLP}\bigl(\mathrm{concat}(\mathcal{C},\, \mathrm{TimestepEmb}(t))\bigr)$$

applied at every ResBlock of the 1D U-Net. The condition vector $\mathcal{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c}$ has $d_c = 64$ per their Implementation Details ("the encoder outputs a feature vector of dimension 64"). That 64-dim vector is the entire VGGT-derived signal injected into the policy — surprisingly compressed.

Q5. How does VGGT-DP get a pooled vector per view?

It doesn't — it's pooled across all views jointly.

This is the part of v1 that needed the most correction. The paper's exact pipeline (§ "VGGT as the Feature Projector"):

  1. VGGT aggregator output: $\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{vggt} = \mathrm{VGGT}_\mathrm{agg}(\mathcal{I}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B\cdot T\,\times\,V\,\times\,(N_p+1)\,\times\,D}$. The $+1$ is the camera token.
  2. Reshape the $V \cdot (N_p+1)$ tokens into a single sequence axis.
  3. Transformer encoder (cross-view, intra-frame, not temporal) over that sequence: $\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{proj} = \mathrm{TransformerEnc}(\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{vggt})$.
  4. Average pool over the joint $V \cdot (N_p+1)$ axis:

$$\bar{\mathcal{T}} = \frac{1}{V\cdot(N_p+1)} \sum_{i=1}^{V\cdot(N_p+1)} \mathcal{T}_\mathrm{proj}[:,i,:] \in \mathbb{R}^{B\cdot T\,\times\,D}$$

The pool sums over both views and tokens — there's no per-view bottleneck. Then $\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{MLP}(\bar{\mathcal{T}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B\cdot T\,\times\,d_c}$ with $d_c = 64$.

So the diffusion policy sees one 64-dim vector per timestep, condensing all VGGT tokens from all cameras at that frame. The paper does not state the number of views $V$ used.

Q6. Does the 1024 include the camera?

Each token (patch, camera, register) is itself a 1024-dim vector. The camera token is one of those 1024-dim vectors.

A short table to keep this straight:

ConceptShapeWhat it represents
One patch token$\mathbb{R}^{1024}$One image patch's features after the aggregator
Camera token$\mathbb{R}^{1024}$A single special token decoded by the camera head into pose
Register tokens (×4)$\mathbb{R}^{1024}$ eachAuxiliary tokens (1 used for register attention sinks, 3 reserved)
Per-frame token bundle$\mathbb{R}^{(N_p+5)\times 1024}$Patches + camera + 4 registers, all in $d=1024$

So "1024" is the token width. Whether the camera token is included in the downstream representation depends on the paper:

  • VGGT-World: yes, kept as part of the $N_p+5$ token bundle, treated indistinguishably from patch tokens by the flow transformer.
  • VGGT-DP: yes — the $+1$ in $(N_p+1)$ is the camera token, and it gets averaged into the final 64-dim condition.
  • MG-Nav: no — only spatial patch tokens are pooled; camera and registers are dropped.
  • Your pipeline: not directly. You feed the decoded camera_pose (9-dim from the camera head) instead of the raw camera token.

2. 🎥 Where the "1024" lives — and whether the camera is in it

One picture in words. After VGGT's aggregator, every frame is encoded as:

per-frame output  =  [ patch_1, patch_2, ..., patch_{N_p}, camera_tok, reg_1, reg_2, reg_3, reg_4 ]
                       └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       (N_p + 5) tokens, each ∈ R^1024

From this same starting point, the four works diverge sharply:

VGGT-World     →  keep all (N_p + 5) tokens at layer 4   →  flow transformer over them
MG-Nav         →  spatial-pool patch tokens only         →  short token sequence (camera/reg dropped)
VGGT-DP        →  Transformer + avg-pool over V·(N_p+1)  →  single 64-dim FiLM condition
Your pipeline  →  ignore tokens, take DPT-decoded outputs →  4116-dim flat (world_points + cam_pose)

All three published works keep the encoder side; you keep the decoded side. That makes your method structurally distinct from the entire concurrent literature — a feature, if you frame it right.

🟢 If VGGT is your selling point, InstanceImageNav is a more natural task

InstanceImageNav lets you feed obs and goal through the same 3D encoder, exploiting cross-image attention naturally. ObjectNav forces a hybrid (image + category embedding), which weakens the "VGGT is the right encoder" argument because part of the conditioning never touches VGGT.

That said: if your contribution is world-model-based ObjectNav (à la DreamerNav / WMNav comparison), staying on ObjectNav and adding a category embedding is the smaller commitment.

5.2 VGGT-World — drop into Related work.tex

📍 Placement: section "World Models for Object Navigation", after the DreamerNav / WMNav discussion at Related work.tex:259. Tone matches your existing third-person, contrastive style.

\paragraph{Geometry-aware autoregressive world models.}
A concurrent line of work explores world modeling directly on top of
3D foundation models. \cite{sun_vggt-world_2026} introduce VGGT-World,
which taps the fourth layer of VGGT's aggregator and treats the resulting
$(N_p+5)\times 1024$ token bundle---spatial patch tokens together with
the camera and register tokens---as a geometry latent state. A
flow-matching transformer is trained autoregressively to forecast future
token chunks, and the remaining frozen aggregator blocks together with
the original DPT heads decode the predicted latents back into depth and
point clouds. In contrast to our method, VGGT-World is action-free: its
flow transformer conditions only on previously rolled-out latent chunks,
and camera trajectories emerge as a side-effect of predicting the
camera-token slot rather than as a controllable input. While they
report that future-prediction training improves present-frame
reconstruction, the absence of action conditioning leaves embodied
control as future work, which our DreamerV3-based formulation directly
addresses.

5.3 MG-Nav — drop into Related work.tex

📍 Placement: section "State of the Art", around Related work.tex:232, in the supervised vs. zero-shot contrast.

\paragraph{Geometry-augmented topological navigation.}
\cite{wang_mg-nav_2025} propose MG-Nav, a dual-scale visual navigator
that combines a hand-built Sparse Memory Graph (SMG) of region-centric
nodes with a frozen Navigation Diffusion Policy. They use VGGT only
as a feature extractor: per-image feature maps are spatially average
pooled and flattened, and a small MLP projects the concatenation
$[\mathbf{F}^G_{\mathrm{obs}} \mid \mathbf{F}^G_{\mathrm{goal}}]$ into
the policy's embedding space. Camera and register tokens, depth maps,
and point maps are all discarded. On HM3D InstanceImageNav, the
VGGT adapter contributes $+4.5$\,SR atop the SMG-augmented baseline,
while the SMG itself contributes $+49$\,SR over the bare policy.
The dominant source of improvement is therefore the topological
prior, not the geometric encoder. Our method differs in two
respects: we operate online without precomputed graphs, and we feed
VGGT's decoded geometry into a learned latent world model rather
than pooling encoder activations through a small adapter.

5.4 VGGT-DP — drop into Related work.tex

📍 Placement: new subsection at Related work.tex:34, before "World Models". The current draft has no diffusion-policy coverage, so this also closes a gap.

\subsection{Vision-Conditioned Policies}
\label{sec:vision-conditioned-policies}

A complementary line of work bypasses world-model learning entirely
and instead conditions a behavior-cloning policy on a frozen vision
encoder. Diffusion policies~\cite{chi_dp_2023, ze_dp3_2024} use a
1D U-Net denoiser to predict short action chunks from the recent
observation window, with image features injected via FiLM
conditioning~\cite{perez_film_2018}. \cite{ge_vggt-dp_2025} replace
the standard ResNet encoder with VGGT: per-frame aggregator tokens
including the camera token are passed through a small Transformer,
average pooled jointly across views and tokens to a single
$d{=}64$ vector, and FiLM conditioned into the U-Net. They evaluate
on MetaWorld with ten demonstrations per task and report wins
on five of ten tasks against Diffusion Policy and DP3, but observe
a sharp viewpoint-OOD failure: success drops from $39\%$ at the
training viewpoint to $0\%$ under a $10^\circ$ camera perturbation.
This brittleness, despite VGGT's geometric pretraining, motivates
our choice to retain the spatial token structure inside a
recurrent latent world model rather than collapse it to a single
condition vector, and to evaluate viewpoint robustness explicitly
in our experiments.
