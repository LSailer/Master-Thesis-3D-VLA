The WP / CP path
how the vggt encoder turns a frame into 4116 numbers

An RGB frame goes into VGGT and comes out as two things — a grid of 3D World Points and one Camera Pose. Stitched together, that is the vector your Dreamer world model actually learns from. This page walks the whole path, end to end, and lets you poke at every stage.

02 — Anatomy

What the 4116 numbers actually are

The adapter does something almost trivial: it reshapes the world points into one long line, then glues the 9 pose numbers onto the end. Hover the two blocks to see how each is built.

$$\underbrace{37 \times 37 \times 3}_{\text{world points} = 4107} \;+\; \underbrace{9}_{\text{camera pose}} \;=\; \mathbf{4116}$$

World Points — 4107

A 37×37 grid of 3D points. Each grid cell stores one \((x,y,z)\) location in space — “where, in the world, is the surface seen through this patch of the image.” Flattening the grid row-major gives 4107 numbers.

$$37\cdot37 = 1369 \text{ patches} \times 3 = 4107$$

vggt_adapter.py:20  out["world_points"].reshape(-1)

Camera Pose — 9

One pose vector in VGGT’s absT_quaR_FoV encoding: 3 translation + 4 quaternion + 2 focal/FoV. It says where the camera is and which way it looks.

$$\underbrace{T_{x,y,z}}_{3} \,\Vert\, \underbrace{q_{x,y,z,w}}_{4} \,\Vert\, \underbrace{f_{x,y}}_{2}$$

camera_head.py:58-61  [T, quat, fl]

Why concatenate instead of fuse? The vggt encoder keeps WP and CP as raw, parallel facts and lets Dreamer’s single dense layer figure out how to use them. No spatial structure is preserved across the flatten — patch \((r,c)\) simply lives at indices \([(r\cdot37+c)\cdot3,\ +3)\). That index map is exactly what you’ll see when you hover the grid in §04.
03 — Camera Pose · CP

The 9 pose numbers, made tangible

Drag the sliders. The top-down map shows where the camera sits in the room \((T_x, T_z)\), the heading derived from the quaternion, and the field-of-view cone set by focal length. This is the entire CP block of the vector.

camera position (T) heading (quaternion) FoV cone (focal)
Translation T  — indices 0,1,2
Quaternion q  — indices 3,4,5,6
Focal / FoV f  — indices 7,8
Quaternion → heading. The 4 quaternion numbers encode rotation without gimbal lock. On the map, only the yaw (turn about the up-axis) is visible, computed as \( \text{yaw} = \operatorname{atan2}\big(2(q_w q_y + q_x q_z),\ 1 - 2(q_y^2 + q_z^2)\big)\). For an agent walking the floor, yaw is the part that matters most.

05 — Into the world model

Where the 4116 vector goes

Once flattened, the vector takes a deliberately short path. The replay buffer stores it as raw float32; at train time the Dreamer-side encoder is a single linear layer — no convolutions, because the spatial reasoning already happened inside VGGT.

The entire Dreamer-side encoder

class VGGTEncoder(nn.Module):       # world_model/encoders.py:40
    embed_dim: int = 1024

    @nn.compact
    def __call__(self, obs):          # obs: (B, 4116) float32
        return nn.Dense(self.embed_dim, name="proj")(obs)

That’s it — one Dense(4116 → 1024). Compare with the CNN encoder, which has to run conv stacks because it receives raw 64×64 pixels instead of pre-computed 3D features.

Design consequence. Because VGGT is frozen and the encoder is linear, every bit of 3D understanding the agent gets is whatever WP/CP already captured. If a useful cue isn't in those 4116 numbers, no amount of Dreamer training can recover it. That is precisely the lever your thesis variants pull on.
06 — Situating it

WP/CP vs. the Aggregator variant

WP/CP is the vggt encoder — it uses VGGT’s output heads. The sibling vggt_aggregator_mlp encoder (Variant 1) throws the heads away and taps the pre-head tokens instead. Knowing the contrast is the fastest way to understand what WP/CP is and isn’t.

One-line mental model. WP/CP = “let VGGT tell me the explicit geometry (points) and where I’m standing (pose), then hand those raw facts to Dreamer.” The aggregator variant = “skip VGGT’s interpretation, take its rich internal features, and let a small MLP re-interpret them.”
